## 1. The question

For the dense-signal Simulation 1B addendum (component `1BD`, M = 5, K = 4, d = 5) and its
frozen d = 3 partner arm: are the DGP parameters — the main-effect array `a` and the
interaction arrays `b` — drawn **once per block** and reused across the 50 replicates, or
drawn **afresh for every replicate**?

This is not a bookkeeping question. Advisor ruling **D13** is binding and reads: *"Use
block-clustered inference on the 8 independent parameter-draw blocks with 7 degrees of
freedom. Use block-resampling bootstrap. Never treat the 48 scenarios or 2,400 replicates
as independent inferential units."* Its justification is a factual assertion about how many
parameter draws exist. If that assertion is false, the ruling's *conclusion* may still be
defensible — but only as an estimand choice, not as a fact about the code.

## 2. The two competing claims

| | claim | consequence if true |
|---|---|---|
| **CLAIM A** (the premise recorded in 01A, in the runner docstring, and hardened into 01B D13) | the block key excludes `delta_eta` and `n_train`, so the 48 scenarios "cluster into 8 parameter-draw blocks"; there are 8 draws | the block is the random unit; n = 8, df = 7; bootstrap resamples blocks |
| **CLAIM B** (raised by a council seat) | `a` and `b` are redrawn for every replicate; there are 8 × 50 = 400 draws per arm, and the cluster is `(block, replicate)`, not `block` | the draw is the random unit; n = 400 per arm, block enters as a fixed effect |

Claim A's *conclusion* that the 48 scenario-level means are not 48 independent observations
is true either way — 6 scenarios do share a draw. What is at issue is the **size of the
cluster and the count of clusters**.

## 5. The disputed between-block variance share

Two prior figures are on the record and they disagree:

- a council seat: *"82% of the clustered variance is fixed-factor heterogeneity"*;
- a later investigation: **14.9%–56.8%**, with the note *"I could not reproduce 82%"*.

**Both are reproducible. They are not the same quantity**, and the earlier "could not
reproduce" was a method mismatch, not a numerical disagreement.

```bash
eval $PY scripts/s0b_d13_premise_probe.py --section variance --reps 50
```

### Method A — exact population, the E1a block-mean contrast (the "82%" route)

Unit: the 8 block-mean d = 5 minus d = 3 contrasts in `relative_log_gap` (hash_shared, B0),
computed exactly from the cell law. `Var` over the 8 block means is split into a draw-noise
component, estimated from the within-block replicate variance of each arm, and a
fixed-factor remainder.

| quantity | A-collapse-first (recommended) | A-delta-independent (as first published) |
|---|---|---|
| SD of the 8 block-mean contrasts | 0.028494 | 0.028494 |
| mean within-block draw SE of a block mean | 0.019098 | 0.012181 |
| implied fixed-factor (condition) SD | 0.021147 | 0.025759 |
| **fixed-factor share of Var(block means)** | **55.1%** | **81.7%** |

The two variants differ in one line. *A-delta-independent* averages the three per-`delta_eta`
replicate variances and then divides again by 3, i.e. it treats the three `delta_eta` levels
as three independent replicates. Section 4.2 measured that they **share one draw exactly**,
so that division is not available: it understates draw noise and inflates the fixed-factor
share. *A-collapse-first* collapses `delta_eta` within a draw first and then varies the
draw, which is the structure the code actually has.

Block means (8 values, printed by the same command):
`[0.0249, −0.01195, −0.01636, −0.01835, −0.00501, −0.01289, −0.07718, −0.02834]`.

## 6. Verdict on the premise

**CLAIM B is correct on the factual question.** `a` and `b` are drawn afresh for every
replicate: **400 independent parameter draws per arm**, not 8. The dependence structure is

```
block  b = 1..8         (marginal x tau x interaction_pairs)   FIXED design factors
  replicate r = 1..50   INDEPENDENT parameter draw   -> 400 draws per arm
    6 scenarios         (3 delta_eta x 2 n_train)    SHARE the draw (b, r)   <- the only cluster
```

The cluster is `(block, replicate)` with 6 members, and there are 400 of them per arm. The
8 blocks are a **complete, exhaustively enumerated 2×2×2 factorial of fixed design factors**
(`run_sim1b_dense_addendum.py:277-279`), not a random sample from a population of blocks;
§5 measures roughly half of their spread to be designed heterogeneity rather than sampling
error. 01A's own `mcse_naming_caveat` — *"the spread across a fixed, exhaustively enumerated
factor grid is designed heterogeneity, not Monte Carlo error"* — applies verbatim one level
up, to the 8 block means.

**Direction of the error.** D13's SE is *conservative in magnitude* for a fixed-grid
estimand: `SE_D13² = (σ²_fixed + σ²_draw)/8 ≥ σ²_draw/8`. Conservatism is not harmless
here, because D16's E1 clauses are **one-sided greater** tests whose
`outcome_classifications` convert a null into a reported conclusion (NOT_SUPPORTED). For
the direction the addendum cares about, an inflated SE manufactures null outcomes. On the
E1a statistic the measured spread between inference units is a factor of 1.5–2.5 in `t`
(see `S0B_NORMALIZED_CONTRAST_SENSITIVITY.md` §"inference unit").

**What survives of D13.** If the advisor *deliberately* wants the estimand to generalize to
a population of conditions beyond this frozen grid, blocks become the random unit and 7 df
is defensible. That is a legitimate **estimand choice** and it must be stated as one. What
cannot stand is the current text, which states it as a fact about the parameter draws and
cites `seed.nunique() == 400` — a count of draws — in support.
